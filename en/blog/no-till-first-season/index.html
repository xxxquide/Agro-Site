2,100 hectares moved to no-till: what the first season showed

Fuel use, moisture retention and yield against conventional tillage. The figures with nothing dressed up.

A first season without deep tillage

We moved 2,100 hectares with the greatest moisture-loss risk to no-till. The purpose was not to showcase a method but to test it in our black soils and real rotation.

Fuel, field time, moisture through the metre profile and yield were compared with neighbouring conventionally tilled fields.

What the figures showed

Fuel use fell and soil retained moisture longer after spring rain. Yield did not rise automatically: compacted zones depended on prior levelling and strong weed control.

  • Fewer machinery passes.
  • Better moisture retention through dry weeks.
  • Greater demand for drill accuracy and weed mapping.

The next step

The method will not be scaled mechanically. Each new field is assessed for compaction, crop history and moisture reserve. For contract-grown crops, the protocol is agreed before drilling.
