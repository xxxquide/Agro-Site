What is actually on offer

Industrial-grade winter rapeseed, 42–45% oil content on a dry matter basis. The seed meets the "double low" specification — canola-type, erucic acid at or below 2% and glucosinolates at or below 18 µmol/g. That is the baseline any crushing plant in Europe accepts without a quality discount; it corresponds to the EU "00" rapeseed convention that international buyers already work to.

The 2026 season closed at 3.6 t/ha — 3,240 tonnes gross from 900 hectares. Rapeseed is the smallest crop we grow by area, just 900 of our 14,200 hectares, but it is the one that opens the harvest campaign every year: the first grain onto our elevator each season is rapeseed, ahead of wheat or maize.

  • Double low — low erucic acid, low glucosinolates, no quality discount from the buyer.
  • Oil content 42–45%, uniform lot, no blending in of weaker fields.
  • No admixture of other oilseeds — fields and equipment are kept crop-dedicated.

Why the smallest crop is the most important one in the rotation

900 hectares is the smallest area in our rotation, but not the smallest role. Rapeseed is the preceding crop for wheat: it clears the field early and leaves behind a loosened, deep soil profile and residual mineral nitrogen that the following winter wheat draws on at establishment. Without rapeseed in the rotation, wheat on the same fields yields lower and less consistently — we have seen this on our own ground whenever the rapeseed area was cut back.

For that reason we would not expand the rapeseed area even against a better price on rapeseed alone: the economics of this crop are not calculated on its own, they are calculated together with the wheat that follows it. It is the most expensive position in the rotation per hectare of input cost, and at the same time the most justified one — it pays itself back through the next crop's yield.

Rapeseed is demanding at establishment: a dry topsoil layer in August and emergence drops, stands come up uneven, and the field goes into winter weak. We plan rapeseed sowing around measured soil moisture with the same discipline we apply to wheat, not around a calendar date.

  • Rapeseed precedes wheat, leaving nitrogen and structured soil behind.
  • Smallest area in the rotation, 900 ha, and the highest sensitivity to sowing timing.
  • Cutting rapeseed area has historically lowered wheat yield on the same fields.

Laboratory and lot certificate

Analysis is done in our own laboratory at the elevator. Every truck coming off the field passes intake control for moisture, impurities and oil content by rapid method. A completed lot then receives a full certificate: oil content, moisture, impurities, erucic acid, glucosinolates, free fatty acids and chlorophyll.

We track free fatty acids and chlorophyll separately from the baseline specification, because those two are what most often let down a rapeseed lot harvested slightly green or over-dried at the elevator. Drying decisions are not made by eye — the operator runs the lot against the laboratory's moisture reading, not against how the seed looks.

The buyer receives the certificate before dispatch. If oil content or purity does not suit, we offer a different lot from a different field — lots are kept separate by field, not blended in one silo.

Harvest, drying, and why rapeseed comes off first

Rapeseed ripens earlier than cereals, and harvest starts 3–4 weeks before wheat. That means the elevator and driers first run at full load on rapeseed — we stress-test all three drying units on the first crop of the season, while tonnage is still manageable, rather than at peak campaign on wheat or maize.

Our own elevator holds 46,000 tonnes; with leased capacity the total is 58,000 tonnes, with drying at 1,200 tonnes per day across three units. Rapeseed is dried more gently than cereal: a high drying temperature quickly raises free fatty acids and degrades oil colour, so rapeseed runs on its own, softer drier setting.

  • Rapeseed harvest opens the season — 3–4 weeks ahead of wheat.
  • A separate, gentler drying setting to avoid raising free fatty acids.
  • 46,000 t own storage, 58,000 t including leased capacity.

Logistics and dispatch

The elevator stands in Soroka, Vinnytsia region: 35 km to Haisyn, 85 km to Vinnytsia, 230 km to Kyiv and 380 km to the port of Odesa. We move rapeseed to dispatch in the first weeks after harvest, before it has spent a season on the elevator and taken up space needed for the next crop coming in.

Dispatch is by road, on your trucks or ours. A lot from 100 tonnes is scheduled for loading within three working days of agreement. Weighbridges are calibrated and certified, and weighing takes place in the presence of the buyer's representative if they wish to attend.

Commercial terms

We work under contract, VAT registered, with a complete document package. Minimum lot is 100 tonnes. Spot payment is 3–5 banking days after acceptance; on a forward, an advance and a payment schedule are fixed in the contract before sowing, already in the summer of the previous year.

Because rapeseed comes off first in the season, we are able to close a forward contract on it earlier than on other crops — tonnage is known before cereal harvest even starts. Since 2008 we have shipped grain and oilseeds to buyers in 14 countries, and the paperwork for every lot is prepared by our own accounts department rather than an intermediary.
